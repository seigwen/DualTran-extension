Side-by-side vs replacement

DualTran and Immersive Translate are not two implementations of the same tool. They are two ways of reading.

Both translate a full web page, both support several translation services, both translate a selected word. The real difference is one sentence long: once the page is translated, is the original text still there?

The whole difference in one table

This table is not meant to prove DualTran is better. Immersive Translate is clearly more mature in breadth of features, PDF support and mobile support; if what you want is “install once, everything works”, it is the better fit. DualTran solves one problem: reading material whose original you need to check again and again.

When side-by-side is better

  • Papers and technical documentation: a dropped not or a mistranslated term leaves you with no way to verify it when the source is gone.
  • Contracts, policies, medical information: you need the exact wording of the original sentence.
  • Learning a foreign language: original on top, translation below — a bilingual reader built into the page.
  • Checking AI translation quality: run Google and an AI on the same page and compare, paragraph by paragraph, which one is closer.

When replacement is better

  • You just want to skim headlines and summaries of the news and do not care about the exact wording.
  • The screen is small (phone, narrow window) and bilingual text would double the page length.
  • You need PDF, e-books, subtitles or other formats that DualTran does not cover today.

Can I use both?

Yes — but two extensions both injecting translations into the page interfere with each other. The practical approach is to split the work by site: use DualTran on the sites where you need to verify the source, and your usual translator everywhere else.
